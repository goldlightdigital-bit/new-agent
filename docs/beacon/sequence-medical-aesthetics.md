# Beacon Sequence — Medical Aesthetics

Full 5-touch cadence for the **medical aesthetics** vertical (med spas, cosmetic-derm and
plastic-surgery practices, aesthetic device brands). This is the sharpest starting ICP:
high-intent local buyers who ask AI "is X a good place for Y treatment," strong YMYL
sensitivity, and visible competitor bleed.

## How to read this

- `«token»` = merge field from the CRM / Signal Engine.
- Every touch stops the sequence if the lead replies, books, or opts out.
- **Touch 1 has two variants.** Use **1a** when the Signal Engine returned a verified
  finding; use **1b** (soft angle, no company claim) when it did not. Never fabricate.
- Keep first touches to 60–90 words. Plain text. One ask.
- Required footer on every email: Gold Light Digital postal address + one-click unsubscribe.

Merge fields used: `«first_name»`, `«practice_name»`, `«treatment»` (their flagship or
highest-margin service), `«city»`, `«top_finding.description»`, `«top_finding.platform»`,
`«competitor_1»`, `«retired_service»`, `«peer_result»`, `«calendar_link»`.

## Touch 1a — Day 0 · Email · verified finding

**Subject:** What «top_finding.platform» tells your «city» patients

> Hi «first_name» — I asked «top_finding.platform» and Perplexity what they'd tell someone
> in «city» researching «treatment».
>
> «top_finding.description» And Perplexity surfaced «competitor_1» above you, citing a
> low-rated review aggregator for your reputation. For a «treatment» practice, that's the
> first impression AI is handing your prospects — before they ever reach your site.
>
> I captured the four screenshots in a one-page snapshot. Worth a 25-minute look at what's
> driving it and what's fixable?
>
> — Kara

*Notes:* `«top_finding.description»` is a full sentence written by the Signal Engine, e.g.
"Gemini recommends a laser service you retired in 2023 and omits your flagship «treatment»."
Do not add severity language beyond what the artifact shows.

## Touch 2 — Day 3 · Email · why it happens & why it matters

**Subject:** Re: what «top_finding.platform» tells your «city» patients

> Following up with the "why," «first_name» — this isn't a search-ranking thing.
>
> AI answer engines summarize whatever they can find and present it as a single confident
> answer. In aesthetics that's high-stakes: it's a YMYL category, the buyer is comparing a
> handful of local names, and the AI's summary is often the *only* research they do. When
> it's outdated or points to «competitor_1», that's revenue walking out the door quietly.
>
> The fix is usually specific and not that heavy. Happy to walk you through what I'd change,
> in 25 minutes. Does this week or next work better?
>
> — Kara

  sources most SEO work never touches, which is why «top_finding.platform» still shows
  «retired_service». Happy to show your agency the snapshot too."

- **"Does AI really drive patients for us?"**
  "It's already in the path — the artifact I mentioned is «city» buyers being handed an
  answer about «practice_name» today. The call is just seeing what it says and whether it's
  worth 25 minutes to fix."

- **"Send me info first."**
  "Better than info — here's your actual snapshot: [attach]. If what it shows is worth
  fixing, the 25 minutes is here: «calendar_link»."

- **"Not the right time."**
  "Totally fair. I'll send the snapshot so you have it, and check back if something changes
  on your end — a launch, a new location, a busy season. Sound good?"
